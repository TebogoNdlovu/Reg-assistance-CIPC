Annual returns guide

CIPC annual returns: the complete guide

What an annual return actually is, exactly when yours is due, what it costs, and the beneficial ownership requirement that now blocks filing if you skip it.

Last updated: September 2026 · 7 min read

An annual return is the single most missed CIPC deadline — not because directors ignore it, but because most don't know the date it's tied to. Here's what it is, when it's due, and what's changed recently.

What an annual return actually is

An annual return is a yearly filing that confirms two things to CIPC: that your company or close corporation is still active, and that its details on the public register are still correct. It is not a tax return, and it has nothing to do with SARS. Filing your ITR14 with SARS satisfies a completely separate obligation.

Every registered entity has to file — companies, close corporations, non-profit companies, and external companies operating in South Africa — whether or not the business traded during the year. A dormant company is not exempt.

When it's actually due

This is where most businesses get caught out. The deadline is not a fixed date shared by every company — it's tied to your own anniversary date, the date your company was originally incorporated (the date on your Certificate of Incorporation).

  • Companies

    Due within 30 business days of your incorporation anniversary each year. Incorporated on 15 March? Your window opens on that date every year and closes roughly six weeks later, depending on public holidays.

  • Close corporations

    Due from the first day of your anniversary month to the end of the following month — a slightly wider window than companies get.

It's not your financial year-end

Directors often assume the annual return lines up with their financial year-end or the tax year. It doesn't. Confusing the two is the single biggest cause of missed filings.

What it costs

CIPC's own fee is based on your company's annual turnover — the higher the turnover, the higher the fee. If you've already missed a year, you'll need to file every outstanding year, not just the most recent one — CIPC won't accept a current filing while prior years are unresolved.

The requirement most directors don't know about: beneficial ownership

Since 1 July 2024, CIPC has enforced a hard stop: an annual return cannot be filed until the company's beneficial ownership (BO) declaration is up to date. If you haven't filed BO, your annual return will not go through, no matter how correct everything else is.

A beneficial owner is any natural person who directly or indirectly holds 5% or more of a company, or who exercises effective control over it, regardless of whether they hold a formal decision-making role. Where a trust holds shares, CIPC requires a "look-through" to the actual people behind it — founders, trustees, and named beneficiaries — since a trust itself can't be listed as the owner.

Filed BO once and think you're done?

Beneficial ownership isn't a once-off filing. It's now reviewed alongside every annual return, so it needs to stay current year to year, not just at registration.

What happens if you don't file

Miss your 30-business-day window and penalty fees start accruing immediately. Miss it for two or more consecutive years, and CIPC begins the deregistration process. A deregistered company loses its legal standing entirely — it can't sign contracts, hold a bank account, or trade lawfully until it's restored.

Already deregistered? Re-instatement

A deregistered company can be restored through a formal re-instatement application (form CoR40.5). As part of that process, every outstanding annual return, along with beneficial ownership and any required financial statements, has to be brought up to date — re-instatement isn't just a form, it's a full compliance catch-up.

The earlier this is dealt with, the simpler it is: a company still in the early notice stage of deregistration is generally more straightforward to restore than one that has been fully struck off the register.

How to file

Annual returns are filed through CIPC's e-Services portal or BizPortal, using your company's customer code and registration details. You'll need your latest turnover figure on hand, and — as above — your beneficial ownership filing needs to already be current.

Frequently asked questions

What is an annual return and do I need to file one?

An annual return is a mandatory yearly filing that confirms your company or close corporation is still active and keeps your CIPC information current. Every registered entity must file one, every year, whether or not it traded.

Is an annual return the same as a SARS tax return?

No. They are entirely separate filings with separate deadlines. Filing your ITR14 with SARS does not file your annual return with CIPC, and filing your CIPC annual return does not satisfy any SARS tax obligation.

Do I need to file if my company didn't trade this year?

Yes. A dormant or non-trading company still has to file its annual return every year. The filing is what tells CIPC the company still exists — it is not conditional on whether the company earned any income.

What happens if I don't file my annual return?

Late penalty fees start accruing once you miss your 30-business-day window. If a company fails to file for two or more consecutive years, CIPC begins the deregistration process, and a deregistered company loses its legal status until it is formally re-instated.

What is beneficial ownership and why does it block my annual return?

Beneficial ownership is a declaration of the natural persons who hold 5% or more interest in a company, or exercise effective control over it. Since 1 July 2024, CIPC will not process an annual return until a company's beneficial ownership filing is up to date.
