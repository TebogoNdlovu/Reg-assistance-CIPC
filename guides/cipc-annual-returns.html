What is a CIPC annual return?

An annual return is a yearly filing that tells the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC) your company or close corporation is still active and that its details on the public register are correct.

It applies to private companies, close corporations, non-profit companies and external companies. It is not a tax return: your SARS filings (such as the ITR14) are completely separate, with different deadlines and different consequences.

Dormant companies are not exempt. A company that didn't trade all year still has to file, because the annual return is how CIPC knows the company still exists.

CIPC annual return deadline: when is yours due?

Your deadline is not a fixed date for everyone, and it is not tied to your financial year-end or tax year. It runs from your incorporation anniversary, the date on your Certificate of Incorporation.

Companies (Pty) Ltd: file within 30 business days after your incorporation anniversary.

Close corporations: file from the first day of your anniversary month until the end of the following month.

The most common mistake

Directors assume the annual return follows the financial year-end. It follows the incorporation anniversary. That mix-up is behind most missed filings.

Beneficial ownership: the step that now blocks your annual return

Since 1 July 2024, CIPC will not accept an annual return until the company's beneficial ownership (BO) filing is up to date. If it isn't, the return is blocked, however correct everything else is.

A beneficial owner is a natural person who holds 5% or more of the company, directly or indirectly, or who exercises effective control over it. Where shares are held through a company or trust, CIPC expects you to look through to the people behind it.

BO is not a once-off job. It has to stay current from year to year, so it is worth checking before every annual return, not just at registration.

What happens if you don't file your annual return?

Late filing attracts penalties. If a company misses its annual returns for two or more consecutive years, CIPC can begin deregistration. A deregistered company loses its legal standing: it can't contract, open or keep bank accounts, or trade lawfully until it is restored.

Deregistration also tends to surface at the worst moment, such as when a bank, a tender or a client asks for proof that the company is in good standing.

Company already deregistered? How reinstatement works

A deregistered company can be restored through a re-instatement application (form CoR40.5). Outstanding annual returns, beneficial ownership and any required financial statements have to be brought up to date as part of the process, so reinstatement is a full compliance catch-up rather than a single form.

The earlier it is dealt with, the simpler it is. A company in the early notice stage is generally easier to restore than one that has been struck off the register.

Frequently asked questions

What is a CIPC annual return?

An annual return is a mandatory yearly filing that confirms your company or close corporation is still active and keeps your details on the CIPC register current. Every registered entity must file one every year, whether or not it traded.

When is my CIPC annual return due?

Companies must file within 30 business days after their incorporation anniversary. Close corporations can file from the first day of their anniversary month until the end of the following month. The deadline follows your incorporation date, not your financial year-end.

How much does a CIPC annual return cost?

CIPC's fee depends on your company's annual turnover bracket. Reg Assistance charges a fixed fee for each bracket. Create a free account on our site to see your price straight away.

Is an annual return the same as a SARS tax return?

No. They are separate filings with separate deadlines. Filing your ITR14 with SARS does not file your CIPC annual return, and filing your annual return does not satisfy any SARS obligation.

Do dormant companies need to file an annual return?

Yes. A company that did not trade still has to file every year. The annual return is what tells CIPC the company still exists, so it does not depend on whether the company earned any income.

What is beneficial ownership and why does it matter for my annual return?

Beneficial ownership is a declaration of the natural persons who hold 5% or more of a company, or who exercise effective control over it. Since 1 July 2024, CIPC will not accept an annual return until the company's beneficial ownership filing is up to date.

What happens if I file my annual return late or not at all?

Late filing attracts penalties. If a company misses its annual returns for two or more consecutive years, CIPC can begin the deregistration process, and a deregistered company loses its legal standing until it is reinstated.

Can a deregistered company be restored?

Yes. A deregistered company can apply for re-instatement using form CoR40.5. Outstanding annual returns, beneficial ownership and any required financial statements need to be brought up to date as part of the process.
